A private research initiative testing one trading method against real market conditions, using virtual capital instead of real money.

Every position is sized, entered, and managed the way a real trade would be — live market prices, real margin requirements, real stop-losses, real drawdown — the only thing that isn't real is the money behind it. The objective is straightforward to state and harder to prove: to sharpen the predictions behind each trade to the point where they reliably compound into asset growth over time — not through a lucky run, but through a documented, repeatable process, tested daily and left open to scrutiny rather than asserted in advance.

System design

How it works

On any given day, predictions may be made across some or all of the ten tracked assets — six major indices and four commodities — sized against a single pooled fund of virtual capital, not real money. There's no obligation to trade every asset daily; how many positions are opened on a given day is a discretionary call, not a fixed requirement.

The portfolio starts from a single pooled fund of $250,000 in virtual capital — the real, spendable balance behind every position. Within that, each of the ten assets carries its own fixed $25,000 baseline, used to track that asset's individual growth and drawdown over time; this is a measurement reference, not a physical division of the cash itself, which remains pooled and available to wherever a position actually needs it.

Predictions are made manually, one trading day ahead, and recorded on video before that day's market session begins. Outcomes are then left to play out against real, live prices — sometimes closed early, sometimes stopped out, sometimes running to the end of the session — exactly as a live account would be managed.

Every result, win or loss, is kept on the record. Nothing is curated after the fact.

Verification

Proof archive

Every prediction video is recorded with an independent cryptographic timestamp — a unique fingerprint, generated at the moment of recording, proving that exact file existed at that exact time. This is independent of any platform involved; the proof is anchored to a public blockchain record that no single party controls.

Proof is not published inline with every video — it exists for each one, and is available on request. The underlying proof for each week is also published here, collectively, by Sunday at the latest. Search by date or date range below to find a specific day's proof.

One thing worth knowing before searching: a prediction video — covering that day's calls across all tracked assets — is always recorded the evening before the trading day it predicts. The date shown in the archive below is the trading date the proof applies to; the matching video will be dated the day prior. For example, proof for a given date's trading corresponds to a video recorded and uploaded the previous evening. This will be simplest to act on once the video channel itself is live, since authenticating a proof requires both the hash and its corresponding video together.

To verify a specific day: find the video recorded the evening before that trading date, then use "View proof" below to get its matching file. That link will open a screen showing "No preview available" — this is expected, not a dead end; the file is a small technical proof document, not something built to be viewed directly. Click "Download" on that same screen to get the file. Upload both — the video and that downloaded file — into any OpenTimestamps-compatible verification tool (opentimestamps.org itself is free and requires no account). It will independently confirm the exact time the video existed, using public blockchain data that no single party controls.

This archive proves a prediction was made and recorded at a specific time — it doesn't by itself show what actually happened afterward.
